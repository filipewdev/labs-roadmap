# Algorithms Track — Overview

> Data structures and algorithms, practised in short, frequent sessions
> instead of weekend labs. The goal is to solve a small, well-defined problem
> quickly, prove it with tests, and explain its trade-offs out loud.

---

## Why a Track and Not a Lab

Weekend labs build systems. Algorithm skill is different: it comes from
repetition, and it fades without use. A single weekend once in a while
doesn't build it; 30–45 minutes three or four times a week does.

So this track runs as a **season**: a block of weeks where drills replace
weekend labs on the board, with one card for the whole season.

---

## The Labs

```
AL-A  Patterns drills          One language · a season of 8–10 weeks
```

More labs may follow (timed practice, a second language), but AL-A is the
whole foundation.

---

## Principles

- **Patterns over problems.** Learn the ~15 patterns that most problems reduce to (two pointers, sliding window, hash maps, binary search, BFS/DFS, dynamic programming and others). A problem is practice for a pattern, not a thing to memorise.
- **Tests, as everywhere else.** Every solution ships with tests, including edge cases (empty input, one element, duplicates, very large input).
- **Complexity is part of the answer.** Every solution states its time and space complexity, and why.
- **Write first, look second.** Give each problem a fixed attempt (20–25 minutes) before reading any solution. If you look, re-solve it from scratch two days later.
- **Out loud.** Explaining your approach while you work is a skill of its own. Practise it.

---

## When to Do It

- **Not alongside a heavy season.** Run it as its own season, when you have steady weekday time.
- **Timing matters.** The skill fades, so schedule the season close to when you'll need it, rather than as early as possible.
- **Keep the muscle warm in between:** a few exercises a week on Exercism, in a language you're learning anyway (Go fits the Go labs).

## Tools and Resources

| Tool | What it's for | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| [NeetCode](https://neetcode.io/roadmap) | A roadmap of 150 problems grouped by pattern, each with a video explanation. Use it as the season's plan | Free roadmap and videos; paid tier optional |
| [LeetCode](https://leetcode.com/) | Where most roadmap problems live; runs your solution against hidden tests | Free tier is enough |
| [Exercism](https://exercism.org/) | Language tracks with exercises and free feedback from volunteer mentors. Good between seasons | Free |
| [Beecrowd](https://judge.beecrowd.com/) | Online judge with problems in Portuguese; a gentler start | Free |
| [Big-O Cheat Sheet](https://www.bigocheatsheet.com/) | One page with the complexity of common data structures and sorting algorithms | Free |
| [VisuAlgo](https://visualgo.net/) | Animations of data structures and algorithms, step by step | Free |
| *Cracking the Coding Interview* (Gayle Laakmann McDowell) | The classic book: fundamentals, patterns and practice problems | Paid |
| *Grokking Algorithms* (Aditya Bhargava) | An illustrated, beginner-friendly introduction to the core algorithms | Paid |
